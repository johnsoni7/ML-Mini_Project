# Decisions

- **2026-11-02 21:15, baseline.** The real baseline is `final_v2` (deployed in `app_model_1000_v2`): validation 79.38%, macro F1 78.87%. Re-running it on validation matches the saved predictions to 1e-14. The brief's 61.4% figure is version 1. `f_long_s0` (78.02%, 180 epochs) is the single-model reference.
- **21:30, no new clips for these signs.** The official training split has 14,644 clips for these 1,000 signs and we hold all of them. The only more data would be other signs. It is not the first thing to try: 25,510 more clips would take about 4.5 hours to process.
- **21:30, class weighting dropped.** Classes hold 11 to 17 training clips, a ratio of 1.5. Weighted losses and balanced sampling have almost nothing to correct.
- **21:35, a clean test.** The 3,983 test clips have been scored twice in earlier sessions, so they are not strictly untouched. The official test split holds 12,161 clips for these signs, of which 8,161 have never been used (the other 17 are clips where no hands were found). They will be downloaded, processed once at the end and scored once. The old 3,983 clips will also be reported, for comparison with earlier numbers.
- **21:50, reference run reused.** Re-training the 180-epoch BiLSTM would cost hours while extraction holds the CPU, and it adds nothing to `f_long_s0`.
- **22:00, smaller convolution models.** 256-channel TCN did not finish one epoch in 8 minutes, so convolution candidates use 128 channels.
- **23:10, pre-specified final training.** Written down before any final model exists: once the configuration is chosen on validation, it is retrained with exactly the same epochs and settings on training plus validation clips, because the learning curve says data is the main limit (5,000 clips 55.5%, 14,626 clips about 78%). The validation-based checkpoint is kept separately. Both are then scored once on the unseen test clips. The deployed model is the train-plus-validation one, and this is decided now, so the test score cannot influence it.
- **00:10, raw extraction stopped at 8,862 clips.** On the 5,000-clip gates, bridging gaps (+0.72, within noise) and blended resampling (+0.11, within noise) gave no measurable gain, and face points were a real loss of 6.77. Finishing training clips would have cost about 2.5 hours of CPU for features with no measured benefit. Staying with 30 x 100 also means no browser feature changes and no new parity risk.
- **00:10, aspect correction without raw data.** The 16:9 correction can be computed from stored 30 x 100 values, because each stored frame already holds both shoulders. Train and validation are almost all 4:3, so they are unchanged.
- **00:30, convolution models were not fairly tested.** Without augmentation, the first TCN stayed at chance while a BiLSTM learned. Normalising before each block and using a 256-unit head with dropout 0.2 let it learn (25.5% training accuracy after 25 epochs on 4,000 clips, against 0.2%). The earlier gate scores for `gate_tcn` and `gate_cnn_attn` reflect this fault, not the architectures, so they are re-run at full length.
- **01:32, two-layer BiLSTM stopped.** At 102 seconds per epoch it would need 5 hours of a 7-hour remainder, and it would about double browser time. It was at 62.5% at epoch 28, with no sign of an advantage.

- 2026-11-03 02:23:31: best model is now exp_015 full_mixup: validation top-1 0.8049 and macro F1 0.7986. Chosen on validation only.

- 2026-11-03 02:24:32: best model is now exp_017 full_mixup_always: validation top-1 0.8212 and macro F1 0.8144 beat exp_015 (0.8049, 0.7986). Chosen on validation only.
- **02:30, compute moved to blending every batch.** `full_mixup_always` (82.12%) beat `full_mixup` (80.49%). Stopped the half-strength 300-epoch and velocity runs, and re-queued velocity and a longer run at the stronger setting, plus a new seed to check stability.
- **03:28, TCN measured in the browser.** A checkpoint of the TCN exported to TensorFlow.js and loaded directly, with 1.32 million parameters and 5.1 MB of files. In Chrome with WebGL it took a mean of 12 ms (p95 13 ms) per prediction, against 114 ms (p95 117 ms) for the deployed two-BiLSTM model, both on the same busy machine. Size is therefore no barrier, and combining TCNs is affordable.
- **03:40, calibration.** Blending every batch leaves the model under-confident (mean confidence 74% against accuracy 82%), so at the app's 0.7 threshold it would show 61% of words where the deployed model shows 69%. Each member's temperature is fitted on validation and built into its last layer. `select_final.py` states the ensemble rule before the outcome is known: greedy on validation, stop when a member adds under 0.3 points, at most 4 members.

- 2026-11-03 03:56:55: best model is now exp_025 full_mixup_always+moretime: validation top-1 0.8315 and macro F1 0.8249 beat exp_017 (0.8212, 0.8144). Chosen on validation only.
- **04:30, early start on one final fit.** The train-plus-validation fit of `exp_022`'s settings started before selection, only to protect the deadline. It uses no result from it, and is discarded if validation selection does not choose `exp_022`. The 96-channel TCN was stopped at epoch 17: at 110 seconds per epoch it could not finish.

- 2026-11-03 04:46:08: best model is now exp_022 full_tcn_fair: validation top-1 0.8432 and macro F1 0.8394 beat exp_025 (0.8315, 0.8249). Chosen on validation only.
- **Final fit length.** `exp_022` ended at 158 of 180 epochs under the time cap, with best epoch 148. Its final fit ran all 180 epochs (18,389 clips), so the two differ slightly. `exp_026` ran all 180 in both.
- **06:36, stopped before the 10-hour limit.** Another candidate would need about 2 hours of training plus a final fit, and could no longer be fairly tested. The test clips have been used once, so any further change would make that score less trustworthy.
